PsiTK.AdaptiveRandomizedSVD — Type
AdaptiveRandomizedSVD(; thresh=1e-6, n_test_vectors=10)

Strategy for compress_coulomb_vertex via an adaptive randomized SVD.

The algorithm approximates the range of the row space of $\Gamma$ (the orbital indices are considered as superindex) through a thin basis $Q$, such that

\[\Gamma \approx \Gamma Q Q^\dagger\]

where $\Gamma$ is a $N_{pp} \times N_G$ and $Q$ a $N_G \times N_F$ matrix. This is done through a stochastic $Q$ and a diagonalization of

\[H = -\tilde \Gamma^\dagger \tilde \Gamma = U \Lambda U^\dagger\]

where $\tilde \Gamma = \Gamma Q$. The compressed $\Gamma$ is then obtained via $\Gamma_\text{compressed} = \tilde \Gamma U$, the effective transformation matrix being $Q U$.

The dimension $N_F$ is found by a preceding adaptive range finder. This finder iteratively increases the columns of $Q$ (i.e. $N_F$) in steps of $2\sqrt{N_{pp}}$ and stops when the error for each of n_test_vectors stochastic test vectors $\omega_i$

\[\varepsilon_i = \Vert (1 - QQ^\dagger)\Gamma^\dagger \omega_i \Vert\]

is smaller than $\sqrt{\text{thresh}}/2$. With $r$ test vectors this estimator bounds the true projection error with probability $1 - 10^{-r}$ [Halko, Martinsson, Tropp, SIAM Rev. 53, 217 (2011), Lemma 4.1]; a single test vector would stop the finder too early in a small fraction of runs.

Cost

With $N_{pp} = N_k^2 N_\text{bra} N_\text{ket}$ orbital pairs of the vertex and $N_F$ the dimension of the range found (at least $2\sqrt{N_{pp}}$, before truncation):

  • time: $O(N_{pp} N_G N_F)$ for the projections of the vertex, plus $O(N_G N_F^2)$ for the orthogonalization of $Q$
  • memory: $O(N_{pp} N_F)$ complex numbers for the projected vertex and the random blocks, plus $N_G N_F$ for $Q$

PsiTK.CanonicalVirtuals — Type

Target for generate_orbitals: canonical virtual orbitals, i.e. the lowest eigenpairs of the mean-field Hamiltonian $h$ of the reference (the Fock operator for Hartree-Fock, the Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian for DFT) in the virtual space. n_orbitals=:all yields the complete virtual plane-wave space.

PsiTK.CoulombGramian — Type
CoulombGramian(; thresh=1e-6)

Strategy for compress_coulomb_vertex through the largest eigenvalues of the Coulomb Gramian

\[H = - \Gamma^\dagger \Gamma = U \Lambda U^\dagger\]

The compressed $\Gamma$ is then obtained via $\Gamma_\text{compressed} = \Gamma U$, where the columns of $U$ are restricted such that $|\lambda|$ exceeds thresh.

Cost

With $N_{pp} = N_k^2 N_\text{bra} N_\text{ket}$ orbital pairs of the vertex:

  • time: $O(N_{pp} N_G^2)$ for the Gramian, $O(N_G^3)$ for its diagonalization and $O(N_{pp} N_G N_F)$ for the rotation
  • memory: $O(N_G^2)$ complex numbers for the Gramian and its eigenvectors, plus $N_{pp} N_F$ for the compressed vertex

PsiTK.DensityFitting — Type
DensityFitting

Density-fitting (resolution-of-identity) factorization of the electron repulsion integrals

\[(pr|qs) \propto \sum_F Γ_{rpF}^∗ \, Γ_{qsF}\]

i.e. the Coulomb vertex $Γ$ together with the auxiliary basis $F$ it is expressed in.

Fields

  • Γ: the Coulomb vertex tensor of shape (Nk, Nbra, Nk, Nket, NF). The auxiliary index runs over the plane waves G_vectors (uncompressed) or over the compressed index
  • G_vectors: the plane waves of the uncompressed vertex
  • kernel_fourier: the interaction kernel evaluated at G_vectors
  • singular_vectors: the transformation of shape (NG, NF) from the plane waves to the compressed auxiliary index, or nothing if uncompressed

See compute_coulomb_vertex and compress_coulomb_vertex.

source
PsiTK.DensitySpecificVirtuals — Type
DensitySpecificVirtuals(; n_orbitals, interaction_kernel)

Target for generate_orbitals: compressed virtual orbitals (Density Specific Virtuals) from the lowest eigenpairs of the generalized eigenvalue problem in the virtual space

\[\mathcal K \varphi = \lambda h \varphi\]

where $\mathcal K$ is the exchange operator of the active occupied orbitals with the DFTK InteractionKernel interaction_kernel (at full strength and uncompressed) and $h$ the mean-field Hamiltonian of the reference (the Fock operator for Hartree-Fock, the Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian for DFT). The kernel has no default, since the orbitals depend on it (see generate_orbitals for taking it from the SCF).

The generated orbitals are NOT orthonormal (they are $h$-orthonormal), and their eigenvalues are the generalized Rayleigh quotients $\lambda_i$, not orbital energies. Use canonicalize_orbitals to obtain orthonormal orbitals with energies of $h$.

Cost

Per operator application to one vector, on top of the solver's cost:

  • $\mathcal K$: $O(N_k N_\text{act} N_r \log N_r)$, two FFTs per active orbital and k-point
  • $h$: $O(N_k N_\text{occ} N_r \log N_r)$ with exact exchange (two FFTs per occupied orbital and k-point), $O(N_r \log N_r)$ without
source

FullDiagonalization()

Dense exact diagonalization (LinearAlgebra.eigen) of the operator in the full plane-wave basis. Yields all eigenpairs at once and is preferable to LOBPCG when a large fraction of the spectrum is requested, e.g. CanonicalVirtuals(n_orbitals=:all).

  • time: $N_\text{pw}$ applications of the operators to build them as dense matrices (see the target's docstring), plus $O(N_\text{pw}^3)$ for the diagonalization
  • memory: $O(N_\text{pw}^2)$ complex numbers for the dense matrices

PsiTK.LOBPCG — Type
LOBPCG(; tol=1e-8, maxiter=1000, extra_fraction=0.03, n_extra_min=13,
       callback=DefaultLobpcgCallback())

Iterative eigensolver (lobpcg from LOBPCGEigensolver.jl) computing only the requested number of lowest eigenpairs. Suited when n_orbitals is small compared to the number of plane waves.

To avoid stalling on a (near-)degenerate multiplet cut by the highest requested eigenpair, the block is enlarged by $\max(n_\text{extra,min}, \lceil f_\text{extra} n \rceil)$ vectors, but only the lowest $n$ eigenpairs must converge (n_conv_check) and are returned.

Fields

  • tol::Float64: the residual tolerance for convergence
  • maxiter::Int: maximum number of iterations before the solver aborts with a warning
  • extra_fraction::Float64: fraction $f_\text{extra}$ of extra block vectors
  • n_extra_min::Int: minimal number $n_\text{extra,min}$ of extra block vectors
  • callback: called with the solver state after each iteration; the default prints the convergence progress, callback=identity silences it

Cost

Per iteration, with $n$ the block size (requested eigenpairs plus extra vectors):

  • time: $n$ applications of the operators (see the target's docstring) plus $O(N_\text{pw} n^2 + n^3)$ for the Rayleigh-Ritz step
  • memory: $O(N_\text{pw} n)$ complex numbers for a few blocks of $n$ vectors
source

PsiTK.canonicalize_orbitals — Method
canonicalize_orbitals(space::OrbitalSpace, hamiltonian; occupation_tol=1e-4)

Diagonalizes the mean-field Hamiltonian hamiltonian (e.g. scfres.ham) in the subspace defined by space.ψ to return canonicalized, orthonormal eigenstates sorted by energy. Its exchange operator, if any, must be uncompressed: an ACE exchange (exxalg=AceExx()) is exact only on the orbitals it was sketched with and is rejected with an ArgumentError.

The occupations of space are redistributed by the aufbau principle: the largest occupations are assigned to the lowest eigenvalues. The Fermi level εF is placed in the middle of the gap between occupied and empty orbitals (kept as is if there is no gap).

The aufbau occupation reproduces the input one only if the diagonalization does not mix orbitals of different occupation, e.g. for a converged SCF canonicalized with its own Hamiltonian. Otherwise, e.g. for DFT orbitals canonicalized with a Fock operator, it defines a different determinant, which is not self-consistent with hamiltonian. A warning is issued if the input density matrix $γ = \sum_i f_i |ψ_i⟩⟨ψ_i|$, expressed in the canonical orbitals, deviates from the aufbau occupation by more than occupation_tol in any element.

A deviation $d$ corresponds to an occupied-virtual mixing angle $θ ≈ d / f$ (with $f$ the occupation, e.g. 2) and changes the energy of the determinant by the order of $θ^2$ times the orbital energy gap. The default thus flags changes beyond those left by an SCF converged to about $10^{-9}$ Hartree in the total energy.

Cost

Per k-point, with $N$ orbitals in space and $N_\text{occ}$ occupied orbitals in the exchange operator of hamiltonian:

  • time: $N$ applications of hamiltonian, $O(N_k N_\text{occ} N_r \log N_r)$ each with exact exchange ($O(N_r \log N_r)$ without), plus $O(N_\text{pw} N^2 + N^3)$ for the subspace matrices, their diagonalization and the rotation
  • memory: $O(N_\text{pw} N)$ complex numbers for the orbitals and their images
source
PsiTK.compress_coulomb_vertex — Method
compress_coulomb_vertex(fitting::DensityFitting, strategy)

Compress the Coulomb vertex along its auxiliary axis into a smaller auxiliary index $F$,

\[Γ_{mn F} = \sum_{G} Γ_{mn G} \, U_{G F}\]

where the columns of the transformation $U$ span the dominant subspace of the Coulomb Gramian $\Gamma^\dagger \Gamma$. How $U$ is determined depends on strategy:

  • CoulombGramian: exact diagonalization of the Gramian
  • AdaptiveRandomizedSVD: randomized range finder followed by diagonalization

Arguments

  • fitting: the DensityFitting from compute_coulomb_vertex; compressing an already compressed fitting accumulates the transformations
  • strategy: the compression strategy, carrying its own threshold

Returns

A DensityFitting with the compressed Γ of shape (Nk, Nbra, Nk, Nket, NF) and the accumulated singular_vectors of shape (NG, NF).

Cost

Those of strategy, see CoulombGramian and AdaptiveRandomizedSVD.

PsiTK.compute_coulomb_vertex — Method
compute_coulomb_vertex(
    basis,
    ψ_bra,
    ψ_ket;
    interaction_kernel,
    Ecut_ratio=2/3,
    callback=identity
)
compute_coulomb_vertex(basis, ψ; kwargs...)
compute_coulomb_vertex(bra_space::OrbitalSpace, ket_space::OrbitalSpace; kwargs...)
compute_coulomb_vertex(space::OrbitalSpace; kwargs...)

Compute the Coulomb vertex

\[Γ_{mn \bm G} = \sqrt{v(\bm G)} \; ρ_{mn \bm G}\]

where $ρ_{mn \bm G}$ are the overlap densities (see compute_overlap_densities) and $v(\bm G)$ is the interaction kernel, e.g. the Coulomb potential

\[v(\bm G) = \frac{4π}{\bm G^2}\]

for all orbitals in ψ_bra and ψ_ket. To restrict the orbitals, pass a subset (e.g. via select_orbitals).

Arguments

  • basis: the PlaneWaveBasis of the orbitals
  • ψ_bra: the bra orbitals per k-point (e.g. occupied orbitals)
  • ψ_ket: the ket orbitals per k-point (e.g. virtual orbitals)
  • ψ: orbitals used as both bra and ket, exploiting the symmetry $Γ_{nm,-\bm G} = Γ_{mn \bm G}^∗$ (see compute_overlap_densities)
  • bra_space, ket_space, space: OrbitalSpaces, a shorthand for passing their basis and ψ
  • interaction_kernel: the DFTK InteractionKernel $v$, required since the vertex depends on it. To use the one of a Hartree-Fock or hybrid SCF, take it from the model (the exact-exchange fraction of a hybrid is stored separately and not part of the kernel), e.g. only(t.kernel for t in scfres.basis.model.term_types if t isa ExactExchange). The evaluated kernel is stored in the result (kernel_fourier), so that the vertex and the potential written by the interfaces always agree
  • Ecut_ratio: cutoff ratio for the vertex (default: 2/3), see compute_overlap_densities
  • callback: called after each orbital pair with (; step, total_steps), e.g. callback=ShowProgress() for a progress bar (default: no output)

Returns

A DensityFitting holding the uncompressed vertex Γ (shape (Nk, Nbra, Nk, Nket, NG)), its G_vectors and the kernel_fourier.

Cost

Those of compute_overlap_densities, which the vertex overwrites in place:

  • time: $O(N_\text{bra} N_\text{ket} N_r \log N_r)$
  • memory: $N_\text{bra} N_\text{ket} N_G$ complex numbers for Γ

PsiTK.compute_delta_integrals — Method
compute_delta_integrals(basis, ψ_particles, ψ_holes, ::Val{:PPHH})

Compute the 4-index Delta integrals (Particle-Particle-Hole-Hole) of the particle orbitals ψ_particles and hole orbitals ψ_holes (per k-point, Gamma-only) on the real space grid of basis.

Mathematical Definition

These integrals represent the 4-orbital pointwise overlap between two particle orbitals and two hole orbitals:

\[\Delta_{abij} = \int d^3r \, \psi_a^*(r) \psi_b^*(r) \psi_i(r) \psi_j(r) \approx \sum_{r} \psi_a^*(r) \psi_b^*(r) \psi_i(r) \psi_j(r) \, \Delta V\]

These quantities naturally emerge when decomposing two-electron Coulomb integrals using resolution of the identity or real-space vertex tensors. The returned tensor has the dimensions (Nvirt, Nvirt, Nocc, Nocc).

Cost

With $N_\text{virt}$ particle and $N_\text{occ}$ hole orbitals:

  • time: $O(N_r N_\text{virt}^2 N_\text{occ}^2)$ for the contraction of the orbital pairs
  • memory: $N_r (N_\text{virt}^2 + N_\text{occ}^2)$ complex numbers for the orbital pairs on the grid. This is the practical limit, e.g. 500 virtual orbitals on a $36^3$ grid need about 190 GB.
source
PsiTK.compute_delta_integrals — Method
compute_delta_integrals(basis, ψ_holes, ::Val{:HH})

Compute the 2-index Delta integrals (Hole-Hole) of the hole orbitals ψ_holes (per k-point, Gamma-only) on the real space grid of basis.

Mathematical Definition

These integrals represent the overlap between two hole orbitals evaluated on the real-space grid:

\[\Delta_{ij} = \int d^3r \, \psi_i^*(r) \psi_j(r) \approx \sum_{r} \psi_i^*(r) \psi_j(r) \, \Delta V\]

For perfectly orthonormal orbitals and an infinitely dense grid, this evaluates to the Kronecker delta $\delta_{ij}$. The grid-based numerical representation is returned.

Cost

With $N_\text{occ}$ hole orbitals:

  • time: $O(N_\text{occ} N_r \log N_r + N_r N_\text{occ}^2)$
  • memory: $N_r N_\text{occ}$ complex numbers for the orbitals on the grid
source
PsiTK.compute_overlap_densities — Method
compute_overlap_densities(basis, ψ_bra, ψ_ket; Ecut_ratio=1.0, callback=identity)
compute_overlap_densities(basis, ψ; kwargs...)
compute_overlap_densities(bra_space::OrbitalSpace, ket_space::OrbitalSpace; kwargs...)
compute_overlap_densities(space::OrbitalSpace; kwargs...)

Compute the overlap densities in reciprocal space

\[ρ_{mn \bm G} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{Ω}} \int_Ω \; \psi_{m}(\bm r)^∗ \psi_{n}(\bm r) \; e^{-i\bm r \bm G} \; d^3 r\]

i.e. the coefficients of $\psi_m^∗ \psi_n$ in the orthonormal plane waves $e^{i\bm r \bm G}/\sqrt{Ω}$ (DFTK's fft convention), for all orbitals in ψ_bra and ψ_ket. To restrict the orbitals, pass a subset (e.g. via select_orbitals).

Arguments

  • basis: the PlaneWaveBasis of the orbitals
  • ψ_bra: the bra orbitals per k-point (e.g. occupied orbitals)
  • ψ_ket: the ket orbitals per k-point (e.g. virtual orbitals)
  • ψ: orbitals used as both bra and ket. Only the upper triangle $m ≤ n$ is computed, the rest follows from $ρ_{nm,-\bm G} = ρ_{mn \bm G}^∗$. This shortcut is taken whenever ψ_bra === ψ_ket.
  • bra_space, ket_space, space: OrbitalSpaces, a shorthand for passing their basis and ψ
  • Ecut_ratio: ratio of the plane-wave cutoff (in energy) for the densities relative to the orbital cutoff basis.Ecut (default: 1.0). Values up to supersampling^2 of the basis (4 for DFTK's default) are allowed since the FFT grid holds products of orbitals exactly up to there; Ecut_ratio=4 therefore yields the exact overlap densities.
  • callback: called after each orbital pair with (; step, total_steps), e.g. callback=ShowProgress() for a progress bar (default: no output)

Returns

A tuple (ρmnG, G_vectors):

  • ρmnG: the overlap densities as a tensor of shape (Nk, Nbra, Nk, Nket, NG).
  • G_vectors: the corresponding plane-wave vectors.

Cost

Γ point, with $N_\text{bra}$, $N_\text{ket}$ the number of orbitals in ψ_bra, ψ_ket:

  • time: $O(N_\text{bra} N_\text{ket} N_r \log N_r)$, two FFTs per orbital pair (half as many pairs for ψ_bra === ψ_ket)
  • memory: $N_\text{bra} N_\text{ket} N_G$ complex numbers for ρmnG
source

PsiTK.generate_orbitals — Method
generate_orbitals(target, occ_space::OrbitalSpace, ham; active=occ_space, solver)

Generate virtual orbitals orthogonal to the occupied space occ_space by solving the eigenvalue problem defined by target with the eigensolver solver.

Arguments

  • target: CanonicalVirtuals, DensitySpecificVirtuals or MaximalExchangeVirtuals
  • occ_space: all occupied orbitals of the reference, which are projected out
  • ham: the DFTK mean-field Hamiltonian of occ_space (the Fock operator for Hartree-Fock, the Kohn-Sham Hamiltonian for DFT), e.g. scfres.ham. Its exchange operator, if any, must be uncompressed: an ACE exchange (exxalg=AceExx()) is exact only on the orbitals it was sketched with and is rejected with an ArgumentError
  • active: the occupied orbitals to correlate, a subset of occ_space (e.g. via select_orbitals for embedding). They define the exchange operator $\mathcal K$ of DensitySpecificVirtuals and MaximalExchangeVirtuals; CanonicalVirtuals ignore them
  • solver: LOBPCG, FullDiagonalization or BlockDavidson. Defaults to LOBPCG(), or FullDiagonalization() for CanonicalVirtuals(n_orbitals=:all)

The interaction kernel of $\mathcal K$ is set by target. To use the one of a Hartree-Fock or hybrid SCF, take it from the model; the exact-exchange fraction of a hybrid is stored separately and not part of the kernel:

kernel = only(t.kernel for t in scfres.basis.model.term_types if t isa ExactExchange)
target = DensitySpecificVirtuals(; n_orbitals=50, interaction_kernel=kernel)

Returns

An OrbitalSpace with target.n_orbitals orbitals per k-point.

Cost

Per k-point, with $N_\text{virt}$ = target.n_orbitals: that of solver (see its docstring), with the operator applications of target (see its docstring), plus $O(N_\text{pw} N_\text{virt}^2)$ time for the initial guess. Exchange-based targets build their exchange operator once for all k-points:

  • time: $O(N_k^2 N_\text{act}^2 N_r \log N_r)$ for the exchange energy DFTK evaluates along
  • memory: $N_k N_\text{act} N_r$ complex numbers for the active orbitals in real space

PsiTK.merge_cc4s_tensor_set — Method
merge_cc4s_tensor_set(set, order)

Merge the components of a tensor set, as returned by read_cc4s_tensor_set, into one tensor. The key of each component has one letter per index, and each letter labels a range of that index; order lists the letters in the order of their ranges. E.g. Cc4s writes the one-particle reduced density matrix as the components "hh", "hp", "ph" and "pp" over holes h and particles p, and order="hp" merges them into the matrix over all states with the holes first, the order of dump_cc4s_files.

Throws an ArgumentError unless the keys are exactly all combinations of the letters in order (e.g. the Coulomb integrals of Cc4s lack blocks related by symmetry), and a DimensionMismatch if the sizes of the components are inconsistent.

Returns

A NamedTuple (; tensor, dimensions, unit, metaData) as from read_cc4s_tensor. Of the dimension types, the unit and the metaData, it keeps what all components agree on; the unit is NaN if they disagree.

Cost

Time and memory are linear in the number of elements of all components.

PsiTK.read_cc4s_tensor — Method
read_cc4s_tensor(folder, name)

Read the tensor name in Cc4s format from the files name.yaml and name.elements in folder, as written by Cc4s or by dump_cc4s_files. For a set of tensors, e.g. the blocks of a density matrix, use read_cc4s_tensor_set.

The indices of the returned array follow the order of the dimensions in the YAML file. Cc4s stores the first index fastest, as Julia does, unless the elements are marked as rowMajor, in which case the array is permuted accordingly.

Throws an ArgumentError if a file is missing, describes a tensor set or uses a format that is not supported (a version other than 100, block-sparse tensors, the halfGrid format of real Coulomb vertices), and a DimensionMismatch if the number of elements in the file does not match the dimensions.

Returns

A NamedTuple (; tensor, dimensions, unit, metaData) with

  • tensor: an Array{Float64} for the scalar type Real64 or an Array{ComplexF64} for Complex64, not scaled by unit;
  • dimensions: a Vector of Dicts, one per index, with its "length" and optionally its "type", e.g. "Hole", "Particle" or "State";
  • unit: the unit of the elements;
  • metaData: the Dict of meta data, empty if the file has none.

Cost

Time and memory are linear in the number of elements, e.g. $N^2 N_F$ complex numbers for the Coulomb vertex.

source

PsiTK.VirtualOrbitalTarget — Type

Union of the virtual-orbital targets accepted by generate_orbitals. A new target is a configuration struct with an n_orbitals field, added here, with methods of _eigenproblems and _is_orthonormal. It documents the cost of one application of its operators in its docstring.

PsiTK.ProjectedShiftedOperator — Type
ProjectedShiftedOperator(base_op, V, shift, penalty)

A wrapper that projects the operator into the orthogonal complement of the subspace spanned by the orthonormal columns of $V$, shifts it there by a constant, and assigns a penalty energy to the subspace itself. Applied to a vector $X$, it computes

\[(1 - V V^\dagger) (A + σ) (1 - V V^\dagger) X + p \, V V^\dagger X\]

with $A$ = base_op, $σ$ = shift and $p$ = penalty. The subspace of $V$ is an exact eigenspace with eigenvalue $p$, even if it is not an invariant subspace of $A$ (e.g. orbitals of a loosely converged SCF), so the eigenvectors in the complement are exactly orthogonal to $V$. A penalty above the wanted part of the spectrum keeps eigensolvers away from the subspace.

PsiTK._compress_coulomb_vertex — Function
_compress_coulomb_vertex(Γ, strategy) -> (Γ_F, U)

Extension point for the compression strategies of compress_coulomb_vertex: compress the vertex Γ of shape (Nk, Nbra, Nk, Nket, NG) along its auxiliary axis to Γ_F = Γ U of shape (Nk, Nbra, Nk, Nket, NF), with the transformation U of shape (NG, NF).

A new strategy is a configuration struct with a method of this function. It documents its cost in its docstring.

PsiTK._eigenproblems — Function
_eigenproblems(target, occ_space, active, ham)

Extension point for virtual-orbital targets: the eigenvalue problem that defines the virtual orbitals of target, as a vector over k-points of (; A, B, ε_offset). The lowest eigenpairs of A φ = λ B φ (B = I for a standard eigenvalue problem) are the wanted orbitals with eigenvalues λ + ε_offset. The eigenvalue problem is solved by _solve, so targets and eigensolvers combine freely (see VirtualOrbitalTarget for adding a target).

The arguments are checked by generate_orbitals:

  • occ_space: all occupied orbitals of the reference, which the operators must push out of the low end of the spectrum
  • active: the occupied orbitals to correlate, a subset of occ_space, which define exchange-based targets
  • ham: the mean-field Hamiltonian of occ_space, with an uncompressed exchange operator

PsiTK._is_orthonormal — Function
_is_orthonormal(target)

Whether the orbitals generated for target are orthonormal. Solutions of a generalized eigenvalue problem (B ≠ I in _eigenproblems) are only B-orthonormal.

source
PsiTK._n_guess — Method
_n_guess(solver, n)

Number of guess vectors solver wants in X0 for computing the lowest n eigenpairs in _solve. Defaults to n; a solver converging better with a larger block (e.g. LOBPCG) returns more.

source
PsiTK._solve — Function
_solve(A, B, X0, n, preconditioner, solver) -> (; λ, X)

Extension point for eigensolvers: the lowest n eigenpairs of A φ = λ B φ (B may be I) with the eigenvalues λ and the eigenvectors as columns of X, starting from the guess X0 with size(X0, 2) ≥ n columns (see _n_guess). The eigenvalue problems come from _eigenproblems.

A new eigensolver is a configuration struct with a method of this function, and optionally of _n_guess. It documents its cost in terms of operator applications in its docstring.
